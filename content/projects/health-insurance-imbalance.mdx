<Lead>
Predicting health insurance coverage on a dataset of 72,458 customer records where only 9.5% are uninsured. On this distribution, standard evaluation metrics fail completely: a zero-rule baseline predicting "insured" for every customer reaches 0.904 accuracy and 0.950 F1. ## Dataset profile & anomaly cleaning

The dataset contains 72,458 records across 15 features with 32,260 missing cells (2.97% of all values). The target column `health_ins` comprises 65,553 insured (90.5%) and 6,905 uninsured (9.5%) customers. An unlabelled holdout set of 804 rows was provided for evaluation.

<Figure src="/projects/health-insurance-imbalance/target.webp" alt="Three panels: target bar chart showing 90.5% True and 9.5% False; income box plots by insurance status; stacked bar chart of insurance rate by housing type." caption="Target distribution and key feature associations. Income box plots show substantial overlap between classes: the median income of insured customers matches the 75th percentile of the uninsured group." />

Exploratory analysis identified two structural anomalies and several redundant features:

**Age distribution placeholders.** `age` spans values from 0 to 120. A histogram reveals a sharp spike at 0, zero observations between 1 and 20, a continuous distribution from 21 to 99, and isolated spikes at 100, 110, 114, and 120. Records at age 0 and above 100 show adult characteristics, averaging 36,700 in income and 1.95 vehicles. 43 records at age 0 and 167 records above age 90 are flagged as employed. Values outside [21, 99] represent placeholder entries and were converted to `NaN` for imputation.

<Figure src="/projects/health-insurance-imbalance/age.webp" alt="Three panels: age histogram highlighting spike at 0 and gap from 1 to 20; bar charts showing mean income and vehicle count per age band with anomalous values at ages 0-5 and 100-105." caption="Age 0 and ages over 100 contain adult financial profiles. All age values outside [21, 99] were set to NaN prior to imputation." />

**Redundant features.** `code_column` is perfectly correlated with `state_of_res` (Cramér's V = 1.00) as a duplicate categorical index and was removed. `rooms` exhibits a uniform distribution across its six discrete levels (~12,000 observations each) with negligible correlation to the target (Cramér's V = 0.0084).

<Figure src="/projects/health-insurance-imbalance/cramers.webp" alt="Cramér's V heatmap of categorical features alongside a flat bar chart of insurance rate by room count." caption="Cramér's V matrix showing perfect correlation between state_of_res and code_column. Room count displays uniform counts and zero association with insurance coverage." />

## Non-random missingness in employment

Missing values occur in two distinct structural blocks. `is_employed` accounts for 25,515 missing entries (35.2% of rows), while 6,745 missing cells are distributed across household variables (`housing_type`, `num_vehicles`, `gas_usage`, `recent_move_b`), representing ~1,686 records missing all household attributes simultaneously.

<Figure src="/projects/health-insurance-imbalance/employment.webp" alt="Two panels: is_employed distribution showing 61.6% True, 35.2% Missing, and 3.2% False; age distribution of missing employment records peaking at ages 21-25 and 66-69." caption="Only 2,313 records are explicitly marked unemployed compared to 25,515 missing entries. The missing employment profile peaks among students (21-25) and retirees (66-69)." />

The 25,515 missing employment values represent Not Missing at Random (NMAR) data. Naively imputing these gaps as `False` would inflate the recorded unemployment rate from 3.2% to 38.4%, distorting a feature where insured rates differ significantly (90.7% for employed vs 75.2% for unemployed). Age profiling indicates that missing employment status concentrates among students (ages 21–25) and retirees (ages 66–69).

To preserve this signal, `is_employed` was encoded as a three-state categorical variable (*employed*, *unemployed*, *unrecorded*) before one-hot expansion. Missing entries in `recent_move_b` were restored to `NaN` to be handled by KNN imputation alongside the household block.

## Feature processing pipeline

The feature engineering pipeline transforms raw input columns into a 19-dimensional continuous matrix through eight sequential operations:

1. **Feature selection**: Remove `custid`, `index`, and redundant `code_column`.
2. **Bounds filtering**: Set `age` outside [21, 99] to `NaN`.
3. **Scaling**: Scale `age` to [0, 10] and encoded `state_of_res` to [0, 1] via min-max scaling.
4. **Log transformation**: Apply `log1p(x - min + 1)` to right-skewed variables (`income`, `gas_usage`).
5. **Encoding**: Label-encode binary/categorical fields (`sex`, `is_employed`, `state_of_res`, `health_ins`).
6. **One-hot expansion**: One-hot encode `marital_status`, `housing_type`, and `is_employed`.
7. **Missing restoration**: Revert `recent_move_b` missing category to `NaN`.
8. **Imputation**: Fit a 5-nearest-neighbor distance-weighted `KNNImputer` over all continuous and indicator columns.

Log transformations mitigate extreme right skew in financial features (`income` ranges from -6,900 to 1,257,000; `gas_usage` reaches 570 against a median of 10), preventing distance calculations in KNN imputation from being dominated by extreme values. Although EDA showed `rooms` had no target correlation, it was retained in the pipeline output.

## Resampling strategy & dataset complexity

Six resampling methods from `imbalanced-learn` were applied to balance the training set:

To avoid selecting a resampler based on downstream model artifacts, candidate datasets were evaluated using feature-based complexity measures from `problexity`: F1 (maximum Fisher's discriminant ratio), F2 (volume of overlap region), F3 (maximum feature efficiency), and F4 (collective feature efficiency).

Five resamplers produced nearly identical complexity metrics (F2 = 0.514). SVM-SMOTE recorded F2 = 0.000, indicating zero feature overlap between classes. Because SVM-SMOTE generates synthetic samples along decision boundary support vectors, it produces an artificially separable feature space. The 131,106-row SVM-SMOTE dataset was selected for model benchmarking.

On balanced data, metric scores align closely within each model. KNN is the exception, yielding high specificity (0.963) alongside lower recall (0.818) due to dense synthetic minority clusters influencing distance voting.

Random Forest (100 trees) achieved the highest overall performance (0.935 accuracy, 0.936 F1, 0.930 specificity). XGBoost placed second (0.914 accuracy), constrained by a grid search cap of `max_depth=7` whereas single unpruned decision trees in Random Forest reached depths of 40. Linear and kernel methods trailed ensemble models by 12 to 17 percentage points.

Refitting the 100-tree Random Forest on all 131,106 balanced observations and evaluating on the 804 holdout records produced the final submission.
